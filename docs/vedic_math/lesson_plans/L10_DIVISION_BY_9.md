# Lesson 10: Special Multiplication

## Snapshot

- Course: Vedic Math
- Lesson code: `L10_DIVISION_BY_9`
- Duration: about 32 minutes
- Source: `Vedic Mathematics Manual | chap_10_special_multiplication.pdf`

## Lesson Objective

- Recognize and apply several special multiplication patterns faster than the general method.
- Choose the correct shortcut from the structure of the numbers instead of forcing one method everywhere.
- Keep place value correct when blocks, complements, and carries are part of the method.

## Teaching Approach

- Special multiplication works because the numbers carry a visible pattern that the board can expose quickly.
- Most shortcuts split the answer into left and right blocks, so digit width and carries must be watched carefully.
- Mental speed improves when the learner first spots the pattern, then performs a short sequence of reliable steps.

## Duolingo Lesson Architecture

- Entry onboarding: ask name, comfortable language, current Vedic Math level, and session goal before starting the mission.
- Mission promise: Recognize and apply several special multiplication patterns faster than the general method.
- Core loop: coach hook -> board model -> read aloud -> Niagh tries -> instant feedback -> quick review or reward.
- Reward loop: Award 10 to 20 XP per exercise group based on the step position in the lesson path. Keep the streak visible on every step and protect it with immediate retries instead of long explanations.
- Review rule: Advance only after the learner can answer the checkpoint and complete one try in the same subtopic. Weak steps should return in the next session as review cards before new content.

### Session Loop A-I

- A. Multiplication by 11 for two-digit numbers:
  Coach opens with: To multiply a two-digit number by eleven, copy the outside digits and place their sum in the middle, carrying if needed.
  Board model: Write 52 x 11, copy 5 and 2 to the outside, then reveal the middle sum 5 + 2.
  Read aloud: Why does fifty-two times eleven become five hundred seventy-two?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Multiplication by 11 for two-digit numbers is now stronger and ready for the next step. Find 23 x 11 and 61 x 11 by saying the middle digit before the full answer.
- B. Multiplication by 11 for longer numbers:
  Coach opens with: For a longer number multiplied by eleven, keep the first and last digits and fill each inside position with the sum of neighboring digits.
  Board model: Write 234 x 11, keep 2 and 4 on the ends, then fill the middle places with 2 + 3 and 3 + 4.
  Read aloud: How do the neighboring-digit sums create 2574 from 234 x 11?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Multiplication by 11 for longer numbers is now stronger and ready for the next step. Compute 423 x 11 and 777 x 11 by filling the inside digits from left to right.
- C. By one more than the one before:
  Coach opens with: When the front parts are the same and the ending parts add to ten or a hundred, multiply the common front by one more and place the product of the endings on the right.
  Board model: Write 43 x 47, split it into front 4 and endings 3 and 7, then compute 4 x 5 and 3 x 7.
  Read aloud: Why does 43 x 47 become 2021?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. By one more than the one before is now stronger and ready for the next step. Use the same rule on 62 x 68 and 204 x 206.
- D. Multiplication by nines:
  Coach opens with: To multiply by a row of nines, reduce the number by one for the left part and use all from nine and the last from ten for the right part.
  Board model: Write 763 x 999, subtract one from 763 for the left side, then complement 763 for the right side.
  Read aloud: How do reduce-by-one and complement together create the answer for 763 x 999?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Multiplication by nines is now stronger and ready for the next step. Work 89 x 99 and 778 x 999 using left part first, then right part.
- E. First by the first and the last by the last, and using the average:
  Coach opens with: Two quick multiplication shortcuts work well here: first-by-first-last-by-last for matching endings, and square-the-average minus the square of the difference for centered pairs.
  Board model: Show 27 x 87 and 29 x 31 side by side, with one board half for each method.
  Read aloud: How do you decide between first-by-first-last-by-last and the average method?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. First by the first and the last by the last, and using the average is now stronger and ready for the next step. Find 69 x 49 with the first-last method and 28 x 32 with the average method.
- F. Special numbers and disguised factors:
  Coach opens with: Some products become easy when you spot a hidden special number such as 101, 201, 111, or 403 inside the factors.
  Board model: Work 23 x 101, 43 x 201, and 24 x 37 by rewriting one factor pair into an easy special number.
  Read aloud: How does spotting a special factor change the multiplication problem before you compute?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Special numbers and disguised factors is now stronger and ready for the next step. Use a hidden special number to solve 93 x 67 and 62 x 39.


## Worked Examples

- `52 * 11 = ?` -> Keep 5 and 2 on the outside and place 5 + 2 in the middle. -> answer: `572`
- `43 * 47 = ?` -> Use one more than the one before: 4 x 5 gives 20 and 3 x 7 gives 21, so the answer is 2021. -> answer: `2021`
- `763 * 999 = ?` -> Reduce 763 by one for the left part and use all from 9 and the last from 10 for the right part. -> answer: `762237`
- `29 * 31 = ?` -> Average 30 and difference 1 give 30 squared minus 1 squared. -> answer: `899`
