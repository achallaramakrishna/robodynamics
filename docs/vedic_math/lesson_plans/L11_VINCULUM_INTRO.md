# Lesson 11: General Multiplication

## Snapshot

- Course: Vedic Math
- Lesson code: `L11_VINCULUM_INTRO`
- Duration: about 34 minutes
- Source: `Vedic Mathematics Manual | chap_11_general_multiplication.pdf`

## Lesson Objective

- Multiply two-digit, long-by-two-digit, and three-digit numbers using a single coherent vertically-and-crosswise pattern.
- Understand how carries are merged in mental left-to-right work and how the same logic appears in written right-to-left work.
- Transfer the same multiplication structure from arithmetic into algebraic binomials.

## Teaching Approach

- General multiplication is not a new collection of tricks; it is one expandable pattern of vertical and crosswise products.
- Carries are handled by merging adjacent result blocks rather than by restarting the calculation.
- The same structure works for digits, grouped digit pairs, and algebraic terms, which makes the method unified and memorable.

### Session Loop A-I

- A. Revision: multiplying by a single digit from left to right:
  Coach opens with: Before general multiplication, revise the left-to-right merging idea for a number times a single digit.
  Board model: Write 74 x 8, compute 7 x 8 and 4 x 8 separately, then merge the inner carry from left to right.
  Read aloud: Why does seventy-four times eight become five hundred ninety-two when we merge the partial products?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Revision: multiplying by a single digit from left to right is now stronger and ready for the next step. Find 73 x 3 and 77 x 4 by speaking the left product, right product, and merged answer.
- B. Two-figure multiplication vertically and crosswise:
  Coach opens with: Two-figure multiplication uses a three-step pattern: vertical on the left, crosswise in the middle, and vertical on the right.
  Board model: Write 21 x 23 one under the other, then reveal the three steps A, B, and C one by one.
  Read aloud: How do the three vertically-and-crosswise steps build the full product for a two-digit multiplication?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Two-figure multiplication vertically and crosswise is now stronger and ready for the next step. Work 14 x 21 and 23 x 41 by naming left, middle, and right parts before the final answer.
- C. Moving multiplier for long numbers times two digits:
  Coach opens with: A two-digit multiplier can move across a longer number, giving one vertical result at each end and crosswise results in between.
  Board model: Write 4321 x 32, slide the 32 across the number position by position, and record each result in order.
  Read aloud: Why does the moving multiplier create five partial results for a four-digit number times a two-digit number?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Moving multiplier for long numbers times two digits is now stronger and ready for the next step. Work 321 x 21 and 31013 x 21 by listing the moving-multiplier results first.
- D. Three-figure extension and pairs of digits:
  Coach opens with: Three-figure numbers extend the same pattern with a five-step symmetry of one product, two products, three products, two products, and one product.
  Board model: Write 504 x 321 and then 304 x 412, showing the five-step pattern and the pair-of-digits variant.
  Read aloud: How does the one-two-three-two-one pattern organize a full three-digit multiplication?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Three-figure extension and pairs of digits is now stronger and ready for the next step. Compute 321 x 321 and 123 x 45 by naming the five steps in order.
- E. Multiplying binomials with the same pattern:
  Coach opens with: The same vertically-and-crosswise structure works for algebraic binomials just as it does for arithmetic numbers.
  Board model: Write (x + 3)(x + 4) and (2x + 5)(3x + 2) in stacked form and fill left, middle, and right parts.
  Read aloud: Why does the exact same multiplication pattern work for binomials and for two-digit numbers?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Multiplying binomials with the same pattern is now stronger and ready for the next step. Expand (x + 5)(x + 6) and (2x - 3)(3x + 4) by saying left, crosswise, and right terms.
- F. Written right-to-left calculations:
  Coach opens with: The same vertically-and-crosswise method can also be written from right to left, placing digits down as soon as each step is completed.
  Board model: Work 42 x 31, 86 x 23, and 234 x 234 from right to left, recording each digit and carry under its step center.
  Read aloud: How does the written right-to-left form match the mental pattern instead of replacing it?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Written right-to-left calculations is now stronger and ready for the next step. Write out 31 x 41 and 231 x 32 from right to left with carries at each stage.


## Worked Examples

- `74 * 8 = ?` -> Compute 56 and 32, then merge the inner carry from 32 into 56 to get 592. -> answer: `592`
- `21 * 23 = ?` -> Use left vertical 4, middle crosswise 8, and right vertical 3 to get 483. -> answer: `483`
- `4321 * 32 = ?` -> Use the moving multiplier to produce 12, 17, 12, 7, and 2, then merge them to 138272. -> answer: `138272`
- `504 * 321 = ?` -> Use the five-step one-two-three-two-one pattern and merge the results to 161784. -> answer: `161784`
