# Lesson 12: Squaring

## Snapshot

- Course: Vedic Math
- Lesson code: `L12_FRACTIONS_DECIMALS`
- Duration: about 34 minutes
- Source: `Vedic Mathematics Manual | chap_12_squaring.pdf`

## Lesson Objective

- Square numbers quickly by choosing the right pattern instead of defaulting to long multiplication.
- Use duplexes and splitting to extend squaring methods from two digits to much larger numbers.
- Check or recover square information through digit clues and square-root reasoning.

## Teaching Approach

- Several special squaring cases are short front-and-back patterns, but they all connect to the same deeper duplex structure.
- When a number sits near a friendly anchor like 50, the anchor and the distance from it do most of the work.
- Even larger squares and algebraic squares follow the same left-middle-right logic, which keeps the lesson unified.

## Duolingo Lesson Architecture

- Entry onboarding: ask name, comfortable language, current Vedic Math level, and session goal before starting the mission.
- Mission promise: Square numbers quickly by choosing the right pattern instead of defaulting to long multiplication.
- Core loop: coach hook -> board model -> read aloud -> Niagh tries -> instant feedback -> quick review or reward.
- Reward loop: Award 10 to 20 XP per exercise group based on the step position in the lesson path. Keep the streak visible on every step and protect it with immediate retries instead of long explanations.
- Review rule: Advance only after the learner can answer the checkpoint and complete one try in the same subtopic. Weak steps should return in the next session as review cards before new content.

### Session Loop A-I

- A. Squaring numbers ending in 5:
  Coach opens with: For a number ending in 5, multiply the part before the 5 by one more than itself, and place 25 on the right.
  Board model: Write 75 squared as 7 and 5, then compute 7 x 8 for the left block and append 25.
  Read aloud: Why does seventy-five squared become fifty-six twenty-five?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Squaring numbers ending in 5 is now stronger and ready for the next step. Square 55, 15, and 95 by saying the left block before the full answer.
- B. Squaring numbers near 50:
  Coach opens with: For numbers near 50, adjust 25 by the amount above or below 50 for the left part, and square that small amount for the right part.
  Board model: Show 53 squared and 47 squared side by side, with one board half for above 50 and one for below 50.
  Read aloud: How do the same near-50 ideas work for both fifty-three squared and forty-seven squared?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Squaring numbers near 50 is now stronger and ready for the next step. Square 56, 44, and 42 by saying the adjustment from 50 first.
- C. General squaring with duplexes:
  Coach opens with: General squaring uses duplexes: outer squares on the ends and doubled products in the middle, merged from left to right.
  Board model: Write 43 squared, compute D(4), D(43), and D(3), then merge 16, 24, and 9.
  Read aloud: How do the duplexes of a two-digit number produce the full square?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. General squaring with duplexes is now stronger and ready for the next step. Square 64, 31, and 23 by naming the three duplexes before the answer.
- D. Number splitting and algebraic squaring:
  Coach opens with: Number splitting lets a long square behave like a smaller one, and the same duplex structure carries directly into algebraic squaring.
  Board model: Show 123 squared as 12 slash 3, then place x plus 5 over itself to show the matching algebraic pattern.
  Read aloud: Why do number splitting and algebraic squaring both still follow the same duplex pattern?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Number splitting and algebraic squaring is now stronger and ready for the next step. Square 123 and 312 by splitting, then explain x plus 5 squared in the same left-middle-right way.
- E. Digit sums of squares and square roots of perfect squares:
  Coach opens with: Digit sums help check squares, and square roots of four-digit perfect squares are found by combining a front-digit estimate with a last-digit test.
  Board model: Show 6889 and 5776, mark the first two digits to estimate the tens place, then test the last digit and choose between the two candidates.
  Read aloud: How do the first digits, last digit, and digit-sum check work together when finding a square root?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Digit sums of squares and square roots of perfect squares is now stronger and ready for the next step. Choose between 83 and 87 for the root of 6889, then choose between 74 and 76 for 5776.
- F. Three and four-figure numbers by longer duplexes:
  Coach opens with: Larger squares use longer duplex chains, but the symmetry is unchanged: the square is the total of its duplexes merged in order.
  Board model: Work 341 squared and 4332 squared by listing all duplexes from the outside inward and then merging them one by one.
  Read aloud: Why does a larger square still reduce to a chain of duplexes rather than a completely new method?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Three and four-figure numbers by longer duplexes is now stronger and ready for the next step. Square 212 and 131 by listing the duplex chain first, then merge to the full answer.


## Worked Examples

- `75 * 75 = ?` -> Use 7 x 8 for the left block and append 25 to get 5625. -> answer: `5625`
- `47 * 47 = ?` -> Use the deficiency 3 from 50: left part 25 - 3 = 22 and right part 3^2 = 09. -> answer: `2209`
- `43 * 43 = ?` -> Use duplexes 16, 24, and 9 and merge them to 1849. -> answer: `1849`
- `83 * 83 = ?` -> This confirms the correct square-root candidate for 6889 by direct squaring. -> answer: `6889`
