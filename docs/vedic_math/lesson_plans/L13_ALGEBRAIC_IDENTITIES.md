# Lesson 13: Equations

## Snapshot

- Course: Vedic Math
- Lesson code: `L13_ALGEBRAIC_IDENTITIES`
- Duration: about 26 minutes
- Source: `Vedic Mathematics Manual | chap_13_equations.pdf`

## Lesson Objective

- Solve linear equations mentally by reversing operations in the correct order.
- Handle decimals, fractions, and bracketed expressions without changing the core method.
- Compress multi-step equation solving into one-line mental solutions and confirm answers by substitution.

## Teaching Approach

- Transpose and apply is an undo method: solve by reversing the operation attached to x.
- When there are several operations, undo them from the outside inward instead of guessing or rearranging randomly.
- Fast mental solving is reliable only when it is paired with a quick substitution check.

## Duolingo Lesson Architecture

- Entry onboarding: ask name, comfortable language, current Vedic Math level, and session goal before starting the mission.
- Mission promise: Solve linear equations mentally by reversing operations in the correct order.
- Core loop: coach hook -> board model -> read aloud -> Niagh tries -> instant feedback -> quick review or reward.
- Reward loop: Award 10 to 20 XP per exercise group based on the step position in the lesson path. Keep the streak visible on every step and protect it with immediate retries instead of long explanations.
- Review rule: Advance only after the learner can answer the checkpoint and complete one try in the same subtopic. Weak steps should return in the next session as review cards before new content.

### Session Loop A-I

- A. One-step equations by transpose and apply:
  Coach opens with: A one-step equation is solved by reversing the operation around x: added numbers are subtracted, subtracted numbers are added, multiplied numbers are divided, and divided numbers are multiplied.
  Board model: Write x + 39 = 70, x - 7 = 8, 3x = 15, and x / 3 = 7 as four mini-board patterns.
  Read aloud: What does transpose and apply mean in one sentence?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. One-step equations by transpose and apply is now stronger and ready for the next step. Solve x + 3 = 10 and 5x = 35 mentally, then say which operation you reversed.
- B. One-step equations with decimals and fractions:
  Coach opens with: The same transpose-and-apply rule still works when the numbers are decimals, mixed numbers, or fractions.
  Board model: Show x - 3.5 = 4.5, x + 1.3 = 5, and x / 7 = 7 with the undo operation written first.
  Read aloud: Why do decimals and fractions not change the equation-solving rule?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. One-step equations with decimals and fractions is now stronger and ready for the next step. Solve x - 3.5 = 4.5 and x / 7 = 7 by undoing the operation in one mental move.
- C. Two-step equations:
  Coach opens with: A two-step equation is solved by undoing the outer operation first and then undoing the operation still attached to x.
  Board model: Work 2x + 3 = 13 and 5x - 4 = 36 in two clear reverse steps on the board.
  Read aloud: Why do two-step equations get undone in reverse order?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Two-step equations is now stronger and ready for the next step. Solve x / 7 + 3 = 5 and 2x / 3 = 4 by naming the first undo and the second undo.
- D. Two-step equations with a full divided expression:
  Coach opens with: When a whole expression like x minus 3 is divided by 4, the division belongs to the whole bracket, so it must be undone before the inside subtraction.
  Board model: Show (x - 3) / 4 = 5 and (x + 4) / 7 = 5 with the outer division undone before the inside shift.
  Read aloud: Why must the division by 4 be undone before the minus 3 in a divided bracket equation?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Two-step equations with a full divided expression is now stronger and ready for the next step. Solve (x - 21) / 10 = 1 and (x + 4) / 7 = 5 by clearing the divisor first.
- E. Three-step equations and brackets:
  Coach opens with: A three-step equation is solved by peeling back one operation at a time, always from the outside toward x.
  Board model: Work 3x / 5 + 4 = 10, (3x + 2) / 4 = 8, and 2(3x + 4) = 38 on the board in reverse-order steps.
  Read aloud: How do you keep the order correct in a three-step equation?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Three-step equations and brackets is now stronger and ready for the next step. Solve 3x / 5 + 4 = 10 and 2(3x + 4) = 38 by naming the three undo steps aloud.
- F. Mental one-line solutions and checking:
  Coach opens with: Once the undo order is clear, many equations can be solved in a single spoken line and checked immediately by substitution.
  Board model: Take a few earlier equations and show the long form first, then compress them into one spoken mental line and a quick check.
  Read aloud: When can an equation be spoken in one line instead of written in separate steps?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Mental one-line solutions and checking is now stronger and ready for the next step. Say the full one-line solution for 5x - 4 = 36 and for (3x + 2) / 4 = 8, then check by substitution.


## Worked Examples

- `10 - 3 = ?` -> This is the transpose step in x + 3 = 10, so x becomes 7. -> answer: `7`
- `13 - 3 = ?` -> This is the first undo in 2x + 3 = 13. After getting 10, divide by 2. -> answer: `10`
- `10 / 2 = ?` -> This is the second undo in 2x + 3 = 13, so x = 5. -> answer: `5`
- `38 / 2 = ?` -> This is the first undo in 2(3x + 4) = 38. Continue with 19 - 4 and then divide by 3. -> answer: `19`
