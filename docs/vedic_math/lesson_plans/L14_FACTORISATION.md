# Lesson 14: Fractions

## Snapshot

- Course: Vedic Math
- Lesson code: `L14_FACTORISATION`
- Duration: about 28 minutes
- Source: `Vedic Mathematics Manual | chap_14_fractions.pdf`

## Lesson Objective

- Add, subtract, multiply, and divide fractions through a connected set of board patterns instead of unrelated rules.
- Handle mixed numbers and shared-factor denominators efficiently before the arithmetic becomes heavy.
- Compare fractions mentally by cross-products and explain why the four operations are structurally related.

## Teaching Approach

- Crosswise thinking builds fraction addition, subtraction, and comparison from the same visual idea.
- A quick simplification before the main step can reduce the arithmetic load without changing the result.
- Multiplication and division of fractions complete the picture, so the learner sees one coherent family of fraction operations.

### Session Loop A-I

- A. Vertically and crosswise for fraction addition and subtraction:
  Coach opens with: Fractions can be added or subtracted immediately by cross-multiplying the numerators with the opposite denominators and then using the product of the denominators underneath.
  Board model: Write 2/3 + 1/7 and 6/7 - 1/4, then draw the crosswise arrows before computing.
  Read aloud: How do the crosswise products create the numerator in fraction addition or subtraction?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Vertically and crosswise for fraction addition and subtraction is now stronger and ready for the next step. Find 2/5 + 1/4 and 6/7 - 1/4 by naming the crosswise products first.
- B. Mixed numbers and cancellation after combining:
  Coach opens with: For mixed numbers, combine the whole parts separately and use the same crosswise fraction method for the fractional parts, then simplify if needed.
  Board model: Show 7 4/5 + 2 1/3, first adding the whole numbers and then combining the fractions crosswise.
  Read aloud: Why is it efficient to separate whole parts and fractional parts in mixed-number addition?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Mixed numbers and cancellation after combining is now stronger and ready for the next step. Work 5 3/5 - 1 3/4 and 4 1/3 - 1 2/5 by handling whole and fractional parts in order.
- C. Simplification when denominators share a factor:
  Coach opens with: When the denominators share a common factor, first divide them by that factor and use the reduced numbers in the crosswise step.
  Board model: Work 5/12 + 7/18 by placing the reduced helper numbers 2 and 3 below the denominators before cross-multiplying.
  Read aloud: How does reducing the denominators first make the crosswise addition easier?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Simplification when denominators share a factor is now stronger and ready for the next step. Use the simplification trick on 1/3 + 4/9 and 3/5 + 3/10.
- D. Comparing fractions by cross-multiplication:
  Coach opens with: To compare fractions, cross-multiply as if subtracting; the larger cross-product tells you which fraction is greater.
  Board model: Compare 4/5, 2/3, and 5/6 by pairwise cross-multiplication and then place them in ascending order.
  Read aloud: Why is cross-multiplying enough to compare two fractions without converting them to decimals?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Comparing fractions by cross-multiplication is now stronger and ready for the next step. Compare 1/3 and 2/5, then order 5/8, 5/6, and 6/7.
- E. Multiplication and division of fractions:
  Coach opens with: Multiplication of fractions is straight across, while division is crosswise because we multiply by the reciprocal.
  Board model: Show 1/2 x 3/4 and 3/4 ÷ 2/5 with arrows that distinguish straight multiplication from crosswise division.
  Read aloud: Why do multiplication and division of fractions look different on the board but still feel closely related?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Multiplication and division of fractions is now stronger and ready for the next step. Multiply 2/3 by 4/5 and divide 5/6 by 3/4.
- F. Unification of operations on fractions:
  Coach opens with: The four fraction operations are unified: addition and subtraction use crosswise numerators over a denominator product, multiplication is straight across, and division is crosswise with a reciprocal.
  Board model: Lay out a four-box summary board for plus, minus, times, and divide using the same pair of fractions so the visual relation is obvious.
  Read aloud: How are the four fraction operations connected rather than being four unrelated rules?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Unification of operations on fractions is now stronger and ready for the next step. Explain the pattern differences between plus, minus, times, and divide using one pair of sample fractions.
