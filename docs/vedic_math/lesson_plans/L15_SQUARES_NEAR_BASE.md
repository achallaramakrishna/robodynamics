# Lesson 15: Special Division

## Snapshot

- Course: Vedic Math
- Lesson code: `L15_SQUARES_NEAR_BASE`
- Duration: about 34 minutes
- Source: `Vedic Mathematics Manual | chap_15_special_division.pdf`

## Lesson Objective

- Perform special division mentally by using the divisor's relation to a nearby base.
- Track running quotient digits, carries, and remainder adjustments without losing place value.
- Check special-division answers by rebuilding the dividend from divisor times quotient plus remainder.

## Teaching Approach

- Special division is driven by the divisor's deficiency from or excess over a base such as 10 or 100.
- The board layout matters: the base decides the remainder split, and the flagged digits control the running corrections.
- A correct special-division answer should always rebuild the dividend when checked.

### Session Loop A-I

- A. Division by 9 for short numbers:
  Coach opens with: For division by 9, the first digit becomes the first quotient digit, and the sum of the digits gives the remainder, with an extra adjustment if the remainder still contains another 9.
  Board model: Write 23 ÷ 9, 66 ÷ 9, and 58 ÷ 9, then show first-digit quotient and digit-sum remainder on the board.
  Read aloud: Why does division by 9 turn the first digit into the quotient and the digit sum into the remainder?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Division by 9 for short numbers is now stronger and ready for the next step. Find 51 ÷ 9 and 66 ÷ 9 by saying the first quotient and the first remainder before adjusting.
- B. Longer numbers and shortcut carries in division by 9:
  Coach opens with: For longer numbers divided by 9, each quotient digit is added to the next dividend digit to build the next quotient digit, and a short cut prevents double figures from piling up.
  Board model: Work 2301 ÷ 9 and 3172 ÷ 9 left to right, first in the full carry style and then with the short cut.
  Read aloud: How does each quotient digit create the next one in longer division by 9?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Longer numbers and shortcut carries in division by 9 is now stronger and ready for the next step. Divide 2301 by 9 and 777 by 9 by speaking each running total before the next digit is written.
- C. Division by 8, 7, and similar divisors below 10:
  Coach opens with: For a divisor below 10, use the gap from 10: double for 8, treble for 7, and so on, adding that multiple of the current quotient digit to the next dividend digit.
  Board model: Write 31 ÷ 8, 211 ÷ 8, and 123 ÷ 7, then mark the deficiency from 10 beside each divisor.
  Read aloud: Why does division by 8 use doubles and division by 7 use triples?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Division by 8, 7, and similar divisors below 10 is now stronger and ready for the next step. Find 31 ÷ 8 and 123 ÷ 7 by naming the deficiency multiplier before the remainder step.
- D. Division by 99, 98, and similar divisors near 100:
  Coach opens with: For divisors near 100, split the dividend into pairs, bring the first pair down, and use either direct addition for 99 or doubled additions for 98 as you move along.
  Board model: Show 121314 ÷ 99 and 121314 ÷ 98 with the dividend split into pairs and the working written two digits at a time.
  Read aloud: Why do we work in pairs of digits when dividing by 99 or 98?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Division by 99, 98, and similar divisors near 100 is now stronger and ready for the next step. Work 121314 ÷ 99 and 121314 ÷ 98 by saying each pair you write down before the remainder.
- E. Divisor below a base number:
  Coach opens with: When the divisor is just below a base like 100 or 10000, flag its deficiency from the base, bring down the leading answer digits, and add the flagged products into the remainder columns.
  Board model: Work 235 ÷ 88 and 31313 ÷ 7887 with the base deficiency written underneath the divisor and the vertical line marking the remainder part.
  Read aloud: How does the flagged deficiency create the remainder when the divisor is below the base?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Divisor below a base number is now stronger and ready for the next step. Use the flagged deficiency method on 235 ÷ 88 and 31313 ÷ 7887, then check by multiplication plus remainder.
- F. Divisor above a base number:
  Coach opens with: A divisor just above the base uses the same layout as the below-base method, but the flagged part is treated as negative, so the running products are subtracted rather than added.
  Board model: Work 1489 ÷ 123 and 2211 ÷ 112 with barred flag digits to show that the corrections are subtractive.
  Read aloud: Why do the flagged digits become negative when the divisor is above the base?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Divisor above a base number is now stronger and ready for the next step. Use the above-base method on 1489 ÷ 123 and then rebuild the dividend to check the answer.
