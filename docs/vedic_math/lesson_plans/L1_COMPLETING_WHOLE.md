# Lesson 1: Completing the Whole

## Snapshot

- Course: Vedic Math
- Lesson code: `L1_COMPLETING_WHOLE`
- Duration: about 28 minutes
- Source: `Vedic Mathematics Manual | chap_1_completing_the_whole.pdf`

## Lesson Objective

- Understand what Vedic Maths is and why it improves confidence.
- Use base-10 completion for faster mental arithmetic.
- Solve near-ten addition and subtraction with clear steps.

## Teaching Approach

- Teach one visual pattern at a time, then checkpoint immediately.
- Use active recall: student states next step before final answer.
- Keep board, voice, and feedback in one continuous loop.

## Duolingo Lesson Architecture

- Entry onboarding: ask name, comfortable language, current Vedic Math level, and session goal before starting the mission.
- Mission promise: Understand what Vedic Maths is and why it improves confidence.
- Core loop: coach hook -> board model -> read aloud -> Niagh tries -> instant feedback -> quick review or reward.
- Reward loop: Award 10 to 20 XP per exercise group based on the step position in the lesson path. Keep the streak visible on every step and protect it with immediate retries instead of long explanations.
- Review rule: Advance only after the learner can answer the checkpoint and complete one try in the same subtopic. Weak steps should return in the next session as review cards before new content.

### Session Loop A-I

- A. Introduction to Completing the Whole:
  Coach opens with: Scene 1: Introduction to Completing the Whole. We focus on friendly numbers and mental structure.
  Board model: Write the chapter theme and show one quick whole: 24 + 26 = 50.
  Read aloud: In one sentence, what does 'completing the whole' mean?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Introduction to Completing the Whole is now stronger and ready for the next step. Micro-practice: explain how 24 + 26 becomes 50 using whole completion.
- B. Pairs That Make 10:
  Coach opens with: Scene 2: Pairs That Make 10. Instant complements build speed.
  Board model: List 1+9, 2+8, 3+7, 4+6, 5+5 and highlight instant recall.
  Read aloud: What pairs with 7 to make 10? What pairs with 4 to make 10?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Pairs That Make 10 is now stronger and ready for the next step. Micro-practice: answer five complement-to-10 prompts in under 20 seconds.
- C. Ten Point Circle:
  Coach opens with: Scene 3: Ten Point Circle. We use visual branches to track patterns.
  Board model: Draw the ten-point circle and label branch families (1,11,21...) and tens branch (10,20,30...).
  Read aloud: On the ten-point circle, which branch does 32 lie on?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Ten Point Circle is now stronger and ready for the next step. Micro-practice: place 12, 25, 38, and 40 on the correct branches.
- D. Completing the Next Ten:
  Coach opens with: Scene 4: Completing the Next Ten. Reach the nearest ten first, then add the remainder.
  Board model: Solve 49 + 5 as 50 + 4 and mark the split on a number jump.
  Read aloud: Solve 49 + 5 by first completing the next ten.
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Completing the Next Ten is now stronger and ready for the next step. Micro-practice: solve 37 + 6 and say how much was used to reach 40.
- E. Deficiency from Ten:
  Coach opens with: Scene 5: Deficiency from Ten. State how far a number is below the next base.
  Board model: Model deficiency statements: 68 is close to 70 and is 2 below; 58 + 7 = 60 + 5.
  Read aloud: Fill in: 68 is close to ___ and is ___ below.
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Deficiency from Ten is now stronger and ready for the next step. Micro-practice: write deficiency statements for 49, 68, and 79.
- F. Mental Addition with Carry:
  Coach opens with: Scene 6: Mental Addition with Carry. Split tens and ones, then recombine calmly.
  Board model: Show 56 + 26 = (50+20) + (6+6) = 70 + 12 = 82.
  Read aloud: Solve 56 + 26 using tens-and-ones split.
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Mental Addition with Carry is now stronger and ready for the next step. Micro-practice: solve 48 + 45 by splitting tens and ones.
- G. Grouping Numbers into Wholes:
  Coach opens with: Scene 7: Grouping Numbers into Wholes. Reorder to create easy whole sums first.
  Board model: Demonstrate 19 + 8 + 1 = (19+1)+8 and 33+28+4+32 = (28+32)+33+4.
  Read aloud: Reorder 19 + 8 + 1 to make a whole first.
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Grouping Numbers into Wholes is now stronger and ready for the next step. Micro-practice: group 6 + 7 + 4 and solve by forming 10 first.
- H. Add Near a Base:
  Coach opens with: Scene 8: Add Near a Base. Add the base value, then adjust by the deficiency.
  Board model: Work 54 + 39 as 54 + 40 - 1 and 66 + 19 as 66 + 20 - 1.
  Read aloud: Solve 54 + 39 using +40 -1.
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Add Near a Base is now stronger and ready for the next step. Micro-practice: solve 33 + 9 using +10 -1.
- I. Subtract Near a Base:
  Coach opens with: Scene 9: Subtract Near a Base. Subtract the base and add back the deficiency.
  Board model: Work 72 - 48 as 72 - 50 + 2 and 55 - 19 as 55 - 20 + 1.
  Read aloud: Solve 72 - 48 using -50 +2.
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Subtract Near a Base is now stronger and ready for the next step. Micro-practice: solve 61 - 38 using -40 +2.


## Worked Examples

- `8 + 7 = ?` -> Base-10 gap: 8+2=10, then 10+5=15 -> answer: `15`
- `37 + ? = 40` -> Units digit 7: gap = 10-7 = 3 -> answer: `3`
- `10 - 7 = ?` -> Complement (Ten Point Circle): 7+3=10 -> answer: `3`
- `43 + 8 = ?  (two paths)` -> Path A: 43+7=50, 50+1=51 | Path B: 43+10=53, 53-2=51 -> answer: `51`
