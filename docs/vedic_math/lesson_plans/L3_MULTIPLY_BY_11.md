# Lesson 3: Digit Sums and the Nine Point Circle

## Snapshot

- Course: Vedic Math
- Lesson code: `L3_MULTIPLY_BY_11`
- Duration: about 35 minutes
- Source: `Vedic Mathematics Manual | chap_3_digit_sum.pdf`

## Lesson Objective

- Find digit sums quickly, even when the reduction takes two steps.
- Use casting out nines to speed up digit-sum work and check arithmetic answers.
- Read the nine point circle and Vedic Square as pattern tools, not just as facts to memorize.

## Teaching Approach

- Digit sums reduce big numbers to one small checking signal.
- Casting out nines makes mental checking faster because 9s and groups totaling 9 do not change the digit sum.
- Patterns around 9 connect reduction, checking, multiplication structure, and visual design.

## Duolingo Lesson Architecture

- Entry onboarding: ask name, comfortable language, current Vedic Math level, and session goal before starting the mission.
- Mission promise: Find digit sums quickly, even when the reduction takes two steps.
- Core loop: coach hook -> board model -> read aloud -> Niagh tries -> instant feedback -> quick review or reward.
- Reward loop: Award 10 to 20 XP per exercise group based on the step position in the lesson path. Keep the streak visible on every step and protect it with immediate retries instead of long explanations.
- Review rule: Advance only after the learner can answer the checkpoint and complete one try in the same subtopic. Weak steps should return in the next session as review cards before new content.

### Session Loop A-I

- A. Adding Digits and Digit Sums:
  Coach opens with: A digit sum is the single digit you get by adding the digits of a number. If the total is still two digits, reduce again.
  Board model: Show 17 -> 1+7=8 and 123 -> 1+2+3=6 in two simple board lines.
  Read aloud: What is the digit sum of 241?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Adding Digits and Digit Sums is now stronger and ready for the next step. Micro-practice: find the digit sums of 13, 171, and 303.
- B. Two-Step Digit Sums:
  Coach opens with: Some numbers need two steps. For 19, first add to get 10, then reduce 10 to 1.
  Board model: Reveal 19 -> 10 -> 1 and 39 -> 12 -> 3 one arrow at a time.
  Read aloud: Reduce 78 to a single digit. What do you get?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Two-Step Digit Sums is now stronger and ready for the next step. Micro-practice: reduce 83, 614, and 5555 to one digit.
- C. The Nine Point Circle:
  Coach opens with: The nine point circle shows that numbers on the same branch share the same digit sum. Ten, nineteen, and twenty-eight all land on the 1 branch.
  Board model: Draw a nine point circle and place 1, 10, 19, and 28 on the same branch.
  Read aloud: If 28 is on the 1 branch, what is its digit sum?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. The Nine Point Circle is now stronger and ready for the next step. Micro-practice: say the digit sums of 10, 19, 28, and 37.
- D. Casting Out Nines:
  Coach opens with: Any 9 can be ignored, and any group of digits that totals 9 can also be ignored. That makes digit sums much faster.
  Board model: Highlight the 9s in 3949, then cross out 2 and 7 in 24701 as a pair that totals 9.
  Read aloud: After casting out nines, what is the digit sum of 3949?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Casting Out Nines is now stronger and ready for the next step. Micro-practice: use casting out nines on 9329 and 24701.
- E. Digit Sum Puzzles:
  Coach opens with: A digit sum puzzle gives you two clues: the digit sum and one more fact. Use both clues together to pin down the answer.
  Board model: Build a two-column table: clue one is the digit sum, clue two is the number pattern.
  Read aloud: A two-digit number has digit sum 8 and both digits are the same. What is the number?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Digit Sum Puzzles is now stronger and ready for the next step. Micro-practice: solve the clues 'digit sum 9, first digit is double the second' and 'digit sum 5, digits differ by 3'.
- F. Digit Sum Check for Addition:
  Coach opens with: To check an addition, do the sum, find the digit sums of the addends, add those digit sums, and compare with the digit sum of the answer.
  Board model: Write 32 + 12 = 44, then below it show 5 + 3 = 8 and DS(44)=8.
  Read aloud: What is 32 + 12?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Digit Sum Check for Addition is now stronger and ready for the next step. Micro-practice: add 77 and 124, then check the answer with digit sums.
- G. Digit Sum Check for Multiplication:
  Coach opens with: For multiplication, find the digit sums of the factors, multiply those digit sums, reduce if needed, and compare with the digit sum of the product.
  Board model: Show 38 * 3 = 114, then 38 -> 11 -> 2, and 2 * 3 = 6, and DS(114)=6.
  Read aloud: What is 38 * 3?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Digit Sum Check for Multiplication is now stronger and ready for the next step. Micro-practice: solve 62 * 4 and compare the digit sum check with the answer.
- H. The Vedic Square and Pattern Design:
  Coach opens with: The Vedic Square is the multiplication table with every entry replaced by its digit sum. Each row produces a visual pattern.
  Board model: Show row D of the Vedic Square as 4, 8, 3, 7, 2, 6, 1, 5, 9 and mark the repeating route.
  Read aloud: In row D of the Vedic Square, what is the third number?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. The Vedic Square and Pattern Design is now stronger and ready for the next step. Micro-practice: read the first four numbers in row E and describe the turn pattern.
- I. Number Nine:
  Coach opens with: Nine is special: every multiple of 9 has digit sum 9, and the 9-times table climbs on the left and falls on the right. That is why the finger trick works so nicely.
  Board model: Write the 9-times table from 9 * 1 to 9 * 12 and underline the digit sums.
  Read aloud: What is 9 * 11?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Number Nine is now stronger and ready for the next step. Micro-practice: give the answers for 9 * 7, 9 * 8, and 9 * 12, then notice the digit sums.


## Worked Examples

- `What is 32 + 12?` -> Add normally to get 44. Then check with digit sums: DS(32)=5, DS(12)=3, and 5+3=8. The answer 44 also reduces to 8, so the sum is consistent. -> answer: `44`
- `What is 365 + 208?` -> First add to get 573. Then reduce the addends: DS(365)=5 and DS(208)=1, so the check total is 6. The answer 573 also reduces to 6. -> answer: `573`
- `What is 38 * 3?` -> Multiply to get 114. Then reduce the factors: DS(38)=2 and DS(3)=3, so 2*3=6. The product 114 also reduces to 6, so the answer is confirmed. -> answer: `114`
- `What is 62 * 4?` -> Multiply to get 248. Then check the digit sums: DS(62)=8 and 8*4=32, which reduces to 5. The product 248 also reduces to 5. -> answer: `248`
