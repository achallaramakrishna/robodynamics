# Lesson 4: Left to Right Arithmetic

## Snapshot

- Course: Vedic Math
- Lesson code: `L4_VERTICAL_CROSSWISE`
- Duration: about 35 minutes
- Source: `Vedic Mathematics Manual | chap_4_left_to_right.pdf`

## Lesson Objective

- Add from left to right and adjust carries mentally.
- Use left-to-right multiplication and doubling-halving for faster products.
- Subtract with look-ahead and confirm answers with digit sums.

## Teaching Approach

- Work from the biggest place value first.
- Hold a running answer in the mind and adjust it when a later column forces a carry.
- Use a quick check after subtraction so speed stays accurate.

## Duolingo Lesson Architecture

- Entry onboarding: ask name, comfortable language, current Vedic Math level, and session goal before starting the mission.
- Mission promise: Add from left to right and adjust carries mentally.
- Core loop: coach hook -> board model -> read aloud -> Niagh tries -> instant feedback -> quick review or reward.
- Reward loop: Award 10 to 20 XP per exercise group based on the step position in the lesson path. Keep the streak visible on every step and protect it with immediate retries instead of long explanations.
- Review rule: Advance only after the learner can answer the checkpoint and complete one try in the same subtopic. Weak steps should return in the next session as review cards before new content.

### Session Loop A-I

- A. Two-digit addition from left to right:
  Coach opens with: Start with the tens because they show the shape of the answer immediately.
  Board model: Show 56 + 67 as 110 and 13, then combine them into 123.
  Read aloud: What is the running total after the tens in 56 + 67?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Two-digit addition from left to right is now stronger and ready for the next step. Solve 45 + 67 and say the tens total first.
- B. Three-digit addition with carries:
  Coach opens with: Now we hold partial answers and let later carries adjust them.
  Board model: Show 187 + 446 as 5, then 12, then 13, and combine them into 633.
  Read aloud: In 187 + 446, what do 5 and 12 become?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Three-digit addition with carries is now stronger and ready for the next step. Solve 819 + 918 by speaking the first two partial totals.
- C. Multiplication from left to right:
  Coach opens with: Multiply the biggest place first and keep extending the answer from the left.
  Board model: Show 236 * 7 with running products 14, 21, and 42, then combine them into 1652.
  Read aloud: What is the first product from the left in 236 * 7?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Multiplication from left to right is now stronger and ready for the next step. Solve 73 * 7 and explain how 49 and 21 become 511.
- D. Doubling and halving for easier products:
  Coach opens with: If one factor doubles and the other halves, the product stays the same.
  Board model: Show 35 * 22 = 70 * 11 = 770 and 35 * 64 = 70 * 32 = 2240.
  Read aloud: If 35 doubles in 35 * 22, what happens to 22?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Doubling and halving for easier products is now stronger and ready for the next step. Turn 15 * 24 into an easier product before multiplying.
- E. Two-digit subtraction from left to right:
  Coach opens with: Before writing the current answer digit, look at the next column.
  Board model: Show 63 - 37. Reduce the first answer digit from 3 to 2 because 3 - 7 cannot be done, then finish with 13 - 7 = 6.
  Read aloud: Why do we write 2 tens instead of 3 tens in 63 - 37?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Two-digit subtraction from left to right is now stronger and ready for the next step. Solve 75 - 28 and name the look-ahead point.
- F. Digit-sum check for subtraction:
  Coach opens with: Check subtraction with digit sums, then stretch the same look-ahead method to longer numbers.
  Board model: Check 69 - 23 = 46 by digit sums, then show 35567 - 11828 building left to right into 23739.
  Read aloud: What is the digit-sum check for 69 - 23 = 46, and where does look-ahead matter in 35567 - 11828?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Digit-sum check for subtraction is now stronger and ready for the next step. Check 56 - 29 = 27 and solve 6363 - 3388.


## Worked Examples

- `56 + 67 = ?` -> 50 + 60 = 110, then 6 + 7 = 13, so 110 + 13 = 123. -> answer: `123`
- `187 + 446 = ?` -> Think 5, then 12, then 13. Combine from the left to get 633. -> answer: `633`
- `35 * 22 = ?` -> Double 35 and halve 22: 35 * 22 = 70 * 11 = 770. -> answer: `770`
- `63 - 37 = ?` -> 6 - 3 gives 3, but 3 - 7 cannot be done, so reduce to 2 and finish with 13 - 7 = 6. -> answer: `26`
