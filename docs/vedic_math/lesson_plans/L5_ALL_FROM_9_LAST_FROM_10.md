# Lesson 5: All from 9 and Last from 10

## Snapshot

- Course: Vedic Math
- Lesson code: `L5_ALL_FROM_9_LAST_FROM_10`
- Duration: about 34 minutes
- Source: `Vedic Mathematics Manual | chap_5_all_from_9_last_from_10.pdf`

## Lesson Objective

- Find complements quickly by using all from 9 and the last from 10.
- Subtract from powers of 10 without ordinary borrowing.
- Handle money-change questions and adjusted bases mentally.

## Teaching Approach

- For a pure base like 10, 100, or 1000, each digit comes from 9 and the last digit comes from 10.
- If the subtrahend is shorter, imagine the missing leading zeros before taking the complement.
- For bases like 600 or 8000, adjust the first answer digit first, then use complements on the remaining digits.

- Review rule: Advance only after the learner can answer the checkpoint and complete one try in the same subtopic. Weak steps should return in the next session as review cards before new content.

### Session Loop A-I

- A. Complements to 10, 100, and 1000:
  Coach opens with: The rule is short: every digit comes from 9, but the last digit comes from 10.
  Board model: Show 10 - 7 = 3, 100 - 64 = 36, and 1000 - 876 = 124 in one left-to-right reveal.
  Read aloud: What complement to 1000 matches 876?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Complements to 10, 100, and 1000 is now stronger and ready for the next step. Solve 100 - 98 and explain why the written complement is 02 even though the value is 2.
- B. Complements when zeros appear in the number:
  Coach opens with: Zeros do not break the rule. Keep applying the complement calmly and let the last place finish the answer.
  Board model: Show 1000 - 470 = 530 and 100000 - 28160 = 71840 with the zero positions highlighted.
  Read aloud: Why does 470 turn into 530 when the base is 1000?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Complements when zeros appear in the number is now stronger and ready for the next step. Solve 1000 - 280 and say which digit stayed 0 in the answer.
- C. Straight subtraction from a base:
  Coach opens with: When the top number is 10, 100, 1000, or more, the whole subtraction collapses into one complement.
  Board model: Show 1000 - 307 = 693 and 10000 - 6523 = 3477 without ordinary borrowing columns.
  Read aloud: What is the answer to 1000 - 307 if you use the complement directly?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Straight subtraction from a base is now stronger and ready for the next step. Solve 1000 - 580 and say the answer in one flow.
- D. Small subtrahends and leading zeros:
  Coach opens with: If the smaller number has fewer digits, pad it with leading zeros in your mind and the formula stays unchanged.
  Board model: Rewrite 1000 - 43 as 1000 - 043 and 10000 - 58 as 10000 - 0058 before solving.
  Read aloud: Why do we imagine 043 in 1000 - 43?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Small subtrahends and leading zeros is now stronger and ready for the next step. Solve 1000 - 7 by first writing the hidden zeros mentally.
- E. Adjusted bases such as 600, 5000, and 8000:
  Coach opens with: For 600 or 8000, fix the leading digit first, then use all from 9 and the last from 10 on the rest.
  Board model: Show 600 - 77 = 523, 5000 - 123 = 4877, and 8000 - 4222 = 3778 with the first digit adjustment boxed.
  Read aloud: In 8000 - 4222, why does the first answer digit become 3?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Adjusted bases such as 600, 5000, and 8000 is now stronger and ready for the next step. Solve 700 - 232 and say the first digit before finishing the rest.
- F. Money and change as complements:
  Coach opens with: Change from a note is the same complement in a real-life story. We just read the answer as rupees and paise or rupees only.
  Board model: Show change from 100 after paying 76, then change from 1000 after paying 864, and connect each to the same complement pattern.
  Read aloud: If an item costs 76 rupees, what change comes from 100 rupees?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Money and change as complements is now stronger and ready for the next step. Find the change from 1000 rupees when the cost is 358 rupees.


## Worked Examples

- `1000 - 876 = ?` -> Take the complement of 876 to 1000: 8 becomes 1, 7 becomes 2, and the last digit 6 becomes 4. So the answer is 124. -> answer: `124`
- `1000 - 470 = ?` -> Use the same complement idea. Think of 470 as 4, 7, 0. The digits become 5, 3, 0, so the answer is 530. -> answer: `530`
- `1000 - 43 = ?` -> First think of 43 as 043. Then take the complement: 0 becomes 9, 4 becomes 5, and 3 becomes 7. So the answer is 957. -> answer: `957`
- `8000 - 4222 = ?` -> Reduce the first digit of 8 by one more than 4, so the first answer digit is 3. Then take the complement of 222 to get 778. So the answer is 3778. -> answer: `3778`
