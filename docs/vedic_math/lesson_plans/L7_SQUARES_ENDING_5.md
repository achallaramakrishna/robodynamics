# Lesson 7: Base Multiplication

## Snapshot

- Course: Vedic Math
- Lesson code: `L7_SQUARES_ENDING_5`
- Duration: about 36 minutes
- Source: `Vedic Mathematics Manual | chap_7_base_multiplication.pdf`

## Lesson Objective

- Multiply numbers near a base by cross-subtracting or cross-adding mentally.
- Handle carries, larger bases, and different bases without long multiplication.
- Square numbers near a base using the same deficiency idea.

## Teaching Approach

- The left part of the answer comes from a cross-step and the right part comes from a vertical product.
- The number of digits kept on the right depends on the base, not on the product size.
- Proportionately and different-base cases only adjust the left side; the right side still comes from the deficiencies.

### Session Loop A-I

- A. Numbers below 10 by deficiencies from 10:
  Coach opens with: For numbers below 10, mark how far each number is below 10, cross-subtract for the left part, and multiply the deficiencies for the right part.
  Board model: Show 7 - 3 and 8 - 2 beside 7 × 8, then cross-subtract and multiply 3 × 2.
  Read aloud: How does 7 × 8 become 56 by the base-10 deficiency method?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Numbers below 10 by deficiencies from 10 is now stronger and ready for the next step. Solve 6 × 7 and explain where the carry comes from.
- B. Numbers just over 10 and table-pattern intuition:
  Coach opens with: For numbers over 10, the signs become plus instead of minus, so we cross-add for the left part and multiply the surpluses for the right part.
  Board model: Show 12 + 2 and 13 + 3 beside 12 × 13, then sketch the 3-6-9 loop on the 9-point circle as a pattern memory cue.
  Read aloud: Why do we cross-add in 12 × 13 instead of cross-subtracting?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Numbers just over 10 and table-pattern intuition is now stronger and ready for the next step. Solve 13 × 14 and then trace the 3-6-9 pattern once on the circle.
- C. Numbers close to 100 with carries:
  Coach opens with: Near 100, the method is the same, but the right side must keep exactly two digits because the base is 100.
  Board model: Show 88 - 12 and 98 - 02, then cross-subtract for 86 and multiply 12 × 2 for 24.
  Read aloud: Why must the right side of 88 × 98 have exactly two digits?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Numbers close to 100 with carries is now stronger and ready for the next step. Solve 89 × 89 and explain how the carry moves from 121.
- D. Larger numbers near 1000 and above the base:
  Coach opens with: Near 1000 or 10000 the board looks the same, but the right side must keep as many digits as the zeros in the base.
  Board model: Show 568 - 432 and 998 - 002, then 1234 + 234 and 1003 + 003 as two examples below the same rule.
  Read aloud: How many digits must stay on the right when the base is 1000?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Larger numbers near 1000 and above the base is now stronger and ready for the next step. Solve 667 × 998 and explain why the right side needs three digits.
- E. Proportionately and different-base multiplication:
  Coach opens with: Sometimes the cleanest route is to double or halve first, or to line the numbers up against different bases and adjust only the left part.
  Board model: Show 47 × 98 becoming 94 × 98 and then halved, then show 213 × 203 with base 200 and 9998 × 94 with different bases aligned by place value.
  Read aloud: Why does proportionate extension change only the left side in 213 × 203?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Proportionately and different-base multiplication is now stronger and ready for the next step. Solve 309 × 104 and 9998 × 94 using the board alignment.
- F. Squaring near a base and method summary:
  Coach opens with: Squaring near a base is the same idea again: reduce or increase by the deficiency, then write the square of that deficiency on the right.
  Board model: Show 96 × 96 as 92 / 16, 1006 × 1006 as 1012 / 036, and 304 × 304 as 3 × 308 / 16.
  Read aloud: How does 96 × 96 become 92 / 16 on the board?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Squaring near a base and method summary is now stronger and ready for the next step. Square 1006 and then 304 using the same board pattern.
