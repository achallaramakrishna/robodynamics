# Lesson 9: Bar Numbers

## Snapshot

- Course: Vedic Math
- Lesson code: `L9_GENERAL_MULTIPLICATION`
- Duration: about 30 minutes
- Source: `Vedic Mathematics Manual | chap_9_bar_numbers.pdf`

## Lesson Objective

- Convert between barred notation and ordinary numbers in both directions.
- Use bar numbers to simplify subtraction and to remove awkward large digits.
- Recognize when bar form makes a later addition, subtraction, multiplication, or division easier.

## Teaching Approach

- A barred digit is negative, so the number is understood as the next place value minus the barred part.
- Splitting after the barred block keeps long bar numbers manageable and visual on the board.
- Bar numbers are a working notation that can simplify later arithmetic before the final answer is converted back.

### Session Loop A-I

- A. Removing a single barred digit:
  Coach opens with: A bar digit means that digit is negative, so two bar one means twenty minus one and therefore nineteen.
  Board model: Write 21 with a bar over the 1, say 'two bar one', then rewrite it as 20 - 1 = 19.
  Read aloud: Why does two bar one stand for nineteen?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Removing a single barred digit is now stronger and ready for the next step. Convert three bar one and four bar two into ordinary numbers.
- B. Splitting after the bar:
  Coach opens with: When the bar appears inside a longer number, split immediately after the barred block and convert each part calmly.
  Board model: Write five one bar three, split it as 51 / bar3, then convert the first part before reattaching the last digit block.
  Read aloud: Why do we split after the barred block when removing a bar number inside a longer number?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Splitting after the bar is now stronger and ready for the next step. Convert five one bar three two and three bar two one five by splitting after the barred part.
- C. Several barred digits together:
  Coach opens with: When several barred digits come together, think of one block being taken away from the next place value and use complements on the barred part.
  Board model: Show seven bar one four as 700 - 14 and five zero bar three as 500 - 3, then connect this to Lesson 5 complements.
  Read aloud: How does seven bar one four turn into six hundred eighty-six?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Several barred digits together is now stronger and ready for the next step. Convert seven bar zero two and four bar two three one into ordinary numbers.
- D. Subtraction by allowing barred digits:
  Coach opens with: In subtraction, each column can be subtracted directly, and any negative column is written as a barred digit before the answer is converted.
  Board model: Set up 444 - 286 vertically, subtract each column straight down, write 2 bar4 bar2, and then convert it to 158.
  Read aloud: Why does direct column subtraction still work after we rewrite negative columns as barred digits?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Subtraction by allowing barred digits is now stronger and ready for the next step. Subtract 6767 - 1908 by writing every column result first and converting only at the end.
- E. Creating bar numbers from ordinary numbers:
  Coach opens with: To create a bar number, push the earlier place value up by one and turn the high tail into its complement.
  Board model: Write 79, 239, and 7689; then rewrite them as 8 bar1, 24 bar1, and 769 bar1 using the board one place at a time.
  Read aloud: Why does seventy-nine become eight bar one?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Creating bar numbers from ordinary numbers is now stronger and ready for the next step. Put fifty-eight, eighty-eight, and six hundred nine into bar form.
- F. Using bar numbers in sums, subtraction, multiplication, and division:
  Coach opens with: Once a number is in a convenient bar form, we can add, subtract, multiply, or divide with smaller digits and convert back only at the end.
  Board model: Work through 29 + 48, 623 - 188, 29 x 3, and 87 / 3 by converting one number into bar form before the operation.
  Read aloud: When is it worth converting to bar form before doing the main operation?
  Student try: Niagh, try this one. Say your first step, then your answer.
  Reward and review: Correct. Using bar numbers in sums, subtraction, multiplication, and division is now stronger and ready for the next step. Use bar form to simplify 29 + 48 and 29 x 3, then explain why the digits became easier.


## Worked Examples

- `20 - 1 = ?` -> This is the plain-number form of two bar one. First show the barred notation, then rewrite it as twenty minus one. -> answer: `19`
- `500 - 33 = ?` -> This removes the bar number five bar three three by splitting the number and subtracting the barred block from the next place value. -> answer: `467`
- `444 - 286 = ?` -> Subtract each column directly to get a temporary bar-form answer, then convert that bar answer into the ordinary result. -> answer: `158`
- `300 - 13 = ?` -> This shows how 287 can be rewritten in bar form as three bar one three, which removes the large digits before later work. -> answer: `287`
